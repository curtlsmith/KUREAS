01 / 10

Systems modeled, and why

Seventeen systems cover every function the plant needs to trip, cool the core, keep inventory, isolate containment and keep its operators in the control room, plus the power and signals those functions depend on. The design is a natural-circulation integral PWR in a steel containment vessel under a reactor pool, with batteries in place of diesels.

Modeling steps

  1. Define the plant
    One 77 MWe integral PWR module: no RCPs, helical-coil SGs, a containment vessel (CNV) submerged in a below-grade reactor pool that is the ultimate heat sink.
  2. Front-line functions first
    ECCS, DHRS, CIS, RPS, CVCS, FWS, UHS and CRV are the systems an event tree asks about, so each gets a SYSTEM WORKS success statement.
  3. Then support systems
    Offsite power, two 480 VAC divisions with BESS, two 125 VDC divisions, four sensor channels and two ESFAS divisions; reached from front-line systems with SUBSYS(...).
  4. Model the wiring
    73 power, MV, DC and control cables are SSCs, so cable failures appear in cut sets with their routed lengths.
  5. Add human actions
    Two SPAR-H HFEs: manual reactor trip (backs up RPS) and manual DHRS actuation (backs up ESFAS).
  6. Document as you go
    Each system has an HTML extended description and knowledge tags: @source, @boundary, @basis, @assumption, @input, @exclusion, @conservatism, @uncertainty, @note, @modeler.

02 / 10

FMEA creation

Every failure mode carries its effects at four levels, so the FMEA table and report are produced by KUREAS-SYS from the same records that the fault-tree logic uses. There is no separate spreadsheet to keep in step.

Modeling steps

  1. Pick failure modes from data
    Each template lists its modes from the NRC-INL 2020 industry averages (FTS, FTR, FTO, FTC, FTOP, SPUR, PLUG, EXRUPT …) with their distributions.
  2. Mark applicability
    Modes that cannot defeat the function in this role are set not applicable rather than deleted, so the decision is visible.
  3. Write the four effects
    Local (the component), System (the train or system), Plant (the safety function), Detection (how and when it is found).
  4. Tie effects to the success logic
    The System effect states the redundancy the HtSW statement models, e.g. "one of three vent paths is lost (one is enough)".
  5. Cover special modes
    Derived SEIS, FIRE and LEAK modes and the CCF events appear with the SSC and are listed in the report sections that go with them.
  6. Generate the FMEA
    Report > FMEA section: one row per SSC failure mode with its effects, data source and basic event name.

03 / 10

Controlled Naming

Controlled Naming builds every SSC name, and so every basic event name, from pick lists in a fixed order. Names stay unique, sort by system and read the same way in every report, cut set and downstream SEQ script.

Modeling steps

  1. Choose the scheme
    SYS_SSCT_CMP_TRN_SSCID: system, SSC type, component type, train, unique SSC ID. Separator "_".
  2. Seed the pick lists
    Abbreviations come from the KUREAS identification lists (SSCT such as VALVE, PUMP, CABLE; CMP such as HOV, MDP, PCBL). Missing ones were added once at the project level.
  3. Turn it on
    Project Settings > Controlled Naming = on. New SSCs are named by choosing list entries, not typing.
  4. Let modes append
    A basic event is the SSC name plus the failure mode: DHRS_VALVE_HOV_A_AV1 + FTO.
  5. Let CCF events collapse
    A CCF event drops the train and unique ID of its members: DHRS_VALVE_HOV_FTO_CCF.
  6. Use names everywhere
    The same names are referenced by the HtSW scripts, the 3D scripts (PART, WIRE, LEAKRATE) and later by SEQ FAILED() and SEISMIC() lines.

04 / 10

Templates

SSCs that share the same failure data share one template. A template holds the failure modes, data and sources, duty (running or standby), test interval, fire frequency, seismic mapping, CCF participation and the P&ID symbol; an SSC links to it and keeps only what is local to it.

Modeling steps

  1. Group by role, not by name
    A standby pump and a running pump of the same type get different templates (TPL_MDP_STANDBY, TPL_MDP_RUNNING) because their modes differ.
  2. Fill the template once
    Modes, means and distributions from NRC-INL 2020; illustrative values marked HSMR-ILLUS with their basis.
  3. Add duty and test data
    Standby templates carry test intervals and a TM unavailability; these drive the 1-year case.
  4. Link the SSCs
    226 of the 228 SSCs link to one of 47 templates. Only the two HFEs stand alone.
  5. Keep locals local
    Per-SSC values stay on the SSC: the pipe or cable length comes from the 3D route; the fire frequency can be overridden (the UAT uses yard bins 27 and 28).
  6. Opt out where needed
    Pipes, tanks, cables, the grid and structures are templates with CCF off.

05 / 10

Seismic analysis

The median-fragility model gives every SSC a derived SEIS failure mode from its component type. The probability uses the lognormal fragility at one ground-motion level and is ANDed with the probability that an earthquake of that size occurs during the exposure period.

Modeling steps

  1. Enter the hazard curve
    Library > Special Failure Modes > Seismic: 11 points from 0.05 g (1E-2 /yr) to 3.0 g (1E-7 /yr), hypothetical site HSMR-77-HAZ-001.
  2. Set the level
    Seismic g-level 0.5 g: a review-level earthquake (about 1.67 × a 0.3 g design basis).
  3. Map component types
    Each component type points at a library row (NUREG/CR-3558 Table 2, NUREG/CR-4659 Table 3-1), e.g. MDP Am 3.19 g, HOV 7.61 g, relay 1.66 g.
  4. Override where needed
    Per-SSC custom fragilities for items with no library row: the offsite grid (ceramic insulators, Am 0.2 g), the pool and the CNV.
  5. Turn on P_EARTHQUAKE
    P = 1 − exp(−λ(a)·T). T = 72 h in the 72-hour file; 2,160 h (90-day restoration) in the 1-year file.
  6. Read the results
    SEIS events flow into cut sets and importance; the report adds the Seismic Failure Table and the hazard curve.

06 / 10

Fire analysis

The location-based fire model gives an SSC a FIRE failure mode from the fire frequency of its source and the room it sits in. The room comes from the 3D model, so moving a part to another room moves its fire exposure.

Modeling steps

  1. Make rooms fire areas
    Every ROOM in the Project 3D script is a fire area; an SSC belongs to the room it is placed IN.
  2. Give templates a source
    NUREG-2169 bin and plant count per template: pumps bin 21 (12), cabinets and buses bin 15 (30), transformers bin 23 (6), DC batteries bin 1 (2), chargers bin 10 (2), MCR switches bin 4 (4), HVAC bin 26 (8).
  3. Handle the unusual
    Li-ion BESS: user frequency 1E-3 /yr, lognormal EF 10 (no NUREG-2169 bin). UAT: yard bins 27 + 28, count 3.
  4. Set the fragility
    Fragility type "room": a fire in the room fails the targets in it.
  5. Set the exposure
    72 h in the 72-hour file; 720 h (30-day restoration) in the 1-year file.
  6. Check the room split
    Control room HVAC trains A and B were moved to separate rooms (CB_HVACA, CB_HVACB) once a shared room showed up in the results.

07 / 10

Leak analysis

Leak rates are attached to pipes, tanks and the pool with LEAKRATE lines in the 3D scripts. A per-foot rate uses the routed length of the pipe, so the leak frequency grows with the run the 3D model draws.

Modeling steps

  1. Route the pipe
    Pipes are drawn as 3D polylines; KUREAS-SYS measures the routed length.
  2. Attach the rate
    LEAKRATE ON <pipe> RATE 2.5E-10 PER HOUR PER FT (NRC-INL 2020 normal-pipe external leakage, gamma 0.5).
  3. Rate tanks per part
    Tanks: 1.2E-7 /h per tank (gamma 0.5). The pool liner: 1.0E-8 /h, EF 10 (illustrative).
  4. Set the time
    MISSION 72 h in the 72-hour file; the restoration time in the 1-year file (pipe 168 h, tank 336 h).
  5. Say what leaks
    FLUID WATER, IGNITES NO: a water leak that cannot start a fire.
  6. Keep rupture separate
    Pipe rupture (EXRUPT) stays a per-foot mode in TPL_PIPE; LEAKRATE adds the derived LEAK mode beside it.

08 / 10

3D visualization

The 3D model is more than a picture. Rooms set fire exposure, routed WIRE and pipe lengths set cable and pipe failure rates and leak frequencies, and collision checks keep the layout physically possible.

Modeling steps

  1. Build the site
    The Project 3D script sets UNITS, the FOOTPRINT, and the buildings as ROOMs: Reactor, Control, Electrical, Auxiliary and Turbine buildings, plus the switchyard and the cooling tower outdoors.
  2. Add the rooms
    16 rooms nested IN their buildings (MCR, I&C A/B, DC A/B, HVAC A/B, 480 V A/B, BESS A/B, 13.8 kV, gallery, CVCS, CCW, FW pumps), plus the pool room.
  3. Place each system
    Each system's 3D script places PARTs IN rooms by SSC name. The reactor pool is a ROOM named like the UHS SSC, so ECCS and DHRS parts sit inside it.
  4. Route the cables
    Cables are WIREs routed through the rooms; their lengths become the local "Pipe length" of each cable's per-foot failure rate.
  5. Resolve collisions
    Check collisions; pumps, breakers and valves were moved until the count reached 0 (cross-system cable crossings allowed by name).
  6. Use it
    The derived room of every SSC (sys3dDerivedJson) feeds the fire model and, later, the PROJECT fire and flood scenarios.

09 / 10

CCF analysis

The beta-factor model adds one common-cause event for each group of identical components in a system. Groups are formed from the templates, so a group exists wherever two or more SSCs share a template and failure mode within a system.

Modeling steps

  1. Choose the model
    Library > Special Failure Modes > Common-Cause Failures: beta factor (alpha factors are also available).
  2. Set beta
    β = 0.05, lognormal EF 3, about 1 − α₁ for a group of two (INL/EXT-21-65527, 2020 update).
  3. Let groups form
    Same template + same mode + same system = one group (e.g. the three HOV vent valves of ECCS).
  4. Opt out passive items
    Pipes, tanks, cables, the grid and structures have CCF off in their templates.
  5. Name the events
    Controlled Naming gives the CCF event name: ECCS_VALVE_HOV_R_FTO_CCF.
  6. Read the dominance
    CCF events lead many systems: control rods (9.3E-6) in RPS, HOV vent valves (6.0E-5) in ECCS, bistables (2.7E-5) in SEN.

10 / 10

The 72-hour and 1-year models

Both files come from one generator and share systems, names, templates and 3D. They answer different questions. The 72-hour file asks whether a system fails during a 72-hour mission after a demand; the 1-year file asks what fraction of a year it is unavailable.

Modeling steps

  1. Why two files
    In 1.9.0 mission times are per failure mode and the seismic and fire exposure periods are project-wide, so the two cases cannot share one file.
  2. 72-hour case
    Rate modes use a 72 h mission with no repair; leaks, fires and earthquakes use a 72 h exposure.
  3. 1-year: running items
    Running and monitored failures use FM4, λτ/(1+λτ), with τ the restoration time (pump 24 h, valve 12 h, transformer 168 h …).
  4. 1-year: standby items
    Standby rate failures use FM3 with their test intervals; mission-time failures of standby components are screened out.
  5. Unchanged in both
    Demand probabilities and TM unavailabilities are the same in both files.
  6. Exposure periods
    Leaks over restoration time (168 h / 336 h), fires over 720 h, earthquakes over 2,160 h.
